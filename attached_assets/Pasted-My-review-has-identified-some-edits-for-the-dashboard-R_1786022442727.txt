My review has identified some edits for the dashboard: 

Remove the “not client-facing” disclaimers, or do not show this build to clients. The Culture Factor page says verbatim “Intended for internal research, not client-facing use” and the Overview says “Intended for internal research use.” A client reading their own product tell them “not for you” is worse than not showing them anything. Either rewrite as proper methodology / limitations copy, or gate this build until it is done.

Split admin and ops tooling off the client-visible surface. Extraction Manager is a live production console (Start Extraction, Reset All No-Match, Update All Companies — with a monthly auto-run visible), reachable from the main nav under the same login as analytical pages. Export Data, Data Status, and Users have the same problem. These belong on a separate internal-only route, or behind an admin role that hides the tab entirely for non-admins. A client should never be one click from triggering a production job.

Reconcile the contradictory coverage figures and confidence formats. Data Status says Culture Analysis is 100% complete (1,942 of 1,942); Extraction Manager says 839 of 1,957 scored with 1,118 pending. These directly contradict each other and both are visible in the client-facing build. Separately, Company Analysis shows confidence as a raw percentage (“27% confidence”) while every other page uses categorical tiers (High / Medium / Low / Insufficient). Pick one and use it everywhere.

Investigate the “Culture Score Over Time” percentile decay. Both companies I checked (3M and Schroders) show the same shape — from roughly the 75th percentile in 2015 to the 10th–15th percentile by 2023–2026 — with no annotation. The identical pattern on two unrelated companies almost certainly means a methodology artefact, not synchronised cultural decline: probable candidates are dictionary recalibration, changing review-volume mix, or a peer-group redefinition applied historically. This chart is the most visually striking element on a company page and it currently misrepresents company-level trends. Root-cause and annotate before external use.

Page-by-page findings
Login. Clean, minimal, appropriately unbranded. No issues.
Overview / home. Browser tab title reads “Glassdoor Review Dashboard — Financial Services Companies” — the first thing a client sees in their tab bar, and it exposes the raw data source in a way that undercuts the “Schroders 12-bipole framework” positioning of the on-page header. KPI cards, methodology narrative, and the GICS filter (which correctly recalculated headers and KPIs live when I switched to Financials) all work well. The About / Methodology / How-it-works content is genuinely well written, but it is all folded into one long home-page scroll — there is no discrete Methodology page a client can be pointed to or bookmark.
Companies. A bare alphabetical list with review count and rating in a fixed-height scrollable box. No search, no sort, no confidence column — despite the Overview’s own copy promising confidence data on this page. At ~1,957 rows this is unusable for lookup. The data is fine; the UI does not expose it.
Company Analysis (tested on 3M and Schroders). Both loads sat on “Loading analysis…” for several seconds — reproducible, not a one-off. Positive: Schroders’ own profile scores near-neutral (+0.02), which is a good non-bias signal for a Schroders-adjacent product. Issues: confidence shown as raw % (see #4); two charts on the same page use different date-axis formats (ISO “2015-12-31” for Culture Score Over Time; “Q2 ’08” for Culture Rating Trend); the sharp final-quarter drop has no data-sparsity caveat despite Q1/Q2 2026 having far fewer reviews than earlier windows. And the percentile-decay artefact flagged in #5.
Culture Dimensions. The bipolar dot-plot (company vs peer average across 12 dimensions with sample size and confidence annotated) is the most distinctive visualisation on the site — preserve and promote it. Issues: the adjacent bar chart shares the same row labels with no divider, which reads at first glance like a rendering bug; and the Dimension Evidence Detail sub-section has its own separate company dropdown that does not sync with the company already chosen at the top of the page. Once re-selected, this reveals the site’s best table (Tier A–D, Scored reviews, Coverage %, Raw score, Shrunk score, Shrink weight, Concentration). This is institutional-grade transparency and should not be buried behind a broken sync.
Culture Factor. Contains the “Intended for internal research, not client-facing use” line (see #1). The underlying table is rigorous — peer group, model used, sector z, sector percentile, reliability tier, percentile SD (bootstrap), Tier A/B dimension count. No search or column-sort on a 1,957-row table; only the global GICS filter narrows it.
Quarterly Trends. Bug: on first arrival the Company dropdown shows a pre-selected value but the chart area says “Select a company…” — the value has to be manually re-selected to trigger the chart. Once triggered, the chart is clean.
Sector Comparison. Interactive bubble chart with X/Y/size dropdowns and no on-chart labels — you cannot identify a peer group without hovering. More important: the All Peer Groups table shows very high R² for very small peer groups — Multi-Utilities 0.98 on 13 companies, Wireless Telecom 0.98 on 11, Media 0.97 on 8, Trading Companies & Distributors 0.96 on 18. Classic small-sample overfitting, shown with no caveat. By contrast Asset Management & Custody Banks — the peer group most relevant to Schroders — shows R² = 0.26 on 19 companies, which is a far more credible number. Either impose a minimum-N threshold before publishing R² or add a small-sample caveat next to the number.
Backtest — the strongest page on the site. The three-scheme comparison (Current / Walkback — labelled “no look-ahead, most honest test” / Average) is exactly the right framing for an institutional audience, and the Important caveats box (look-ahead, survivorship, no transaction costs, price-only, “descriptive sanity check, not investable performance”) is the best piece of writing in the product. Preserve this pattern and lift its tone into the rest of the site. Small typo: one peer-group row is labelled lowercase “global” against proper-cased labels elsewhere.
Export Data, Data Status, Extraction Manager, Users. All belong behind an admin role (see #2 and #3). The one useful client-facing element buried in Data Status is the data-vintage information (reviews span Jan 2008 – Jul 2026; extraction Jan–Aug 2026; 79.5% of 2,442 companies actually have review data). Surface a “data as of [date]” badge on the Overview and on every company page and disclose the 79.5% coverage gap explicitly — burying it on an ops page is a compliance risk if a client asks.
Mobile / responsive. Fails completely at 390 × 844 (iPhone-class):  document.documentElement.scrollWidth  stayed at 1,377 px regardless of viewport, header and nav overflow off the right with no scroll affordance. Clients open links on phones. At minimum add a “best viewed on desktop” splash on narrow viewports; ideally add responsive breakpoints.
Strengths to preserve
•	Backtest caveats box — set this tone across the rest of the site.
•	Dimension Evidence Detail table — genuine differentiator vs generic culture scores.
•	GICS filter mechanics — Peer Group / Sector / Industry / Sub-Industry switching works cleanly and updates KPIs live.
•	Walkback weighting option on the backtest — signals methodological self-awareness.
•	Schroders’ own score is neutral, not flattering — good non-bias signal for a Schroders-adjacent product.
•	The plain-English “How Culture Scores Are Built” panel on the Overview — a rare good translation of a technical methodology.
