About This Dashboard

This dashboard analyses employee reviews published on Glassdoor to extract and visualise organisational culture signals for companies in the financial services sector. It covers 2,442 MSCI-listed companies spanning 11 GICS sectors and 73 industries, plus 14 major unlisted asset managers, giving a universe of institutions from global investment banks and insurers to boutique asset managers and fintech firms.

Culture is scored using the Schroders v2 framework — twelve bipolar dimensions scored from −1 to +1 with a data-mined dictionary — applied consistently across all companies so that direct, apples-to-apples comparisons are possible. Dimension scores are shrunk toward sector averages where evidence is thin, and combined into a single sector-relative Culture Factor with an explicit reliability tier per company. Intended for internal research use.

How the Analysis Works

STEP 1
Data Collection

Employee reviews are fetched from Glassdoor via the OpenWeb Ninja API (with a RapidAPI fallback). Each review captures the free-text pros, cons and advice fields alongside structured ratings for overall score, culture, work-life balance, compensation, career opportunities, management and diversity.

STEP 2
Keyword Scoring

Each review is scored against the Schroders v2 mined dictionary using a regex engine with negation handling. Every dimension is bipolar: matched terms pull the score towards −1 or +1. Scores are computed per review, averaged to company level, then shrunk toward the sector average in proportion to how thin the evidence is.

STEP 3
Confidence Grading

Each company is assigned a confidence level based on its review count: High (> 50 reviews), Medium (20–50 reviews) or Low (< 20 reviews). Low-confidence scores should be treated as directional only. The dashboard displays the confidence indicator throughout.

STEP 4
Performance Linkage

Financial metrics (ROE, operating margin, TSR, revenue growth) are sourced from FMP for listed companies and from proprietary data for unlisted asset managers. A composite performance score is computed within peer groups (same GICS sector) and linked to culture scores for correlation analysis.

GICS Filtering

The GICS filter in the top-right header controls which companies are included in every chart across the dashboard. GICS (Global Industry Classification Standard) is a four-level hierarchy: Sector → Industry → Sub-Industry → Company. You can filter to a broad Sector (e.g. "Financials"), a narrower Industry (e.g. "Capital Markets") or a precise Sub-Industry (e.g. "Asset Management & Custody Banks"). Selecting "All" restores the full universe. Most analysis tabs update instantly when the filter changes.

The Schroders v2 Culture Framework

The only active framework. Twelve bipolar dimensions scored from −1 to +1 using a dictionary mined from the review corpus itself. The sign indicates which pole dominates; zero means balanced. Earlier Hofstede and MIT frameworks have been retired from active analysis (they remain in the rollback snapshot).

Evidence tiers (A–D)Every company × dimension score carries a tier based on how many reviews actually scored that dimension and how concentrated the evidence is.
Sector-relative shrinkageThin evidence is pulled toward the sector average, so small-sample companies can't dominate rankings on noise.
Culture FactorA single sector-relative score per company, combined with ridge-model weights fit against a composite financial-performance target within each peer group.
Reliability tiersHigh / Medium / Low / Insufficient — reflecting evidence depth and bootstrap rank stability. Low and Insufficient scores are indicative only.

Reading This Chart

  • X-axis (Slope): How much composite performance changes per unit of culture score. Positive = stronger culture alignment → better performance; Negative = inverse relationship.
  • Y-axis (R²): How much of the variation in business performance is explained by the culture score within that group (0 = no fit, 1 = perfect fit).
  • Culture scores use the same correlation-weighted method as the Culture vs Performance tab: each dimension is weighted by its correlation with performance within the group, then multiplied by the company's deviation from the group average.
  • Top-right groups: strong, positive culture-performance link — culture investment likely pays off here.
  • Top-left groups: strong negative link — higher culture scores associate with lower performance in that group (worth investigating why).
  • Bottom groups: weak/no relationship — performance is driven by factors other than culture in these groups.
  • Dot size reflects the number of companies with both culture and performance data. Larger dots = more reliable estimates.
  • Faded grey dots are not statistically significant (p ≥ 0.05); treat them as suggestive only.

Activity Log

Update All Companies — Fetch New Reviews Only

Fetches only reviews posted since the last extraction for every company that already has a Glassdoor company ID. Pages are requested in most-recent-first order; the job stops for each company as soon as two consecutive pages contain entirely known review IDs, so it only downloads what is genuinely new.

Culture Score Processing

Processes extracted review text through the Hofstede, MIT Big 9 and Schroders keyword analysis to generate culture dimension scores. "Process Culture Scores" only scores reviews that have no scores yet. To back-fill newly added dimensions (e.g. Schroders) onto reviews scored earlier, use "Re-score everything" — it clears existing scores first, then re-processes every company. Scoring is local keyword matching; no external API calls are made.
